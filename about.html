The Journey

A life spent inside
how wisdom moves.

EdTerm wasn't built from a business school thesis. It was built from twenty years of firsthand observation — inside the oldest wisdom transfer systems in the world, and inside the communities where that transfer was breaking down.

Age 16
The Apprenticeship

Where wisdom transfer begins

At 16, I entered a four-year Japanese culinary apprenticeship — one of the oldest living systems of knowledge transfer in the world. The most valuable knowledge was never written down. It lived in instinct, in the relationship between a master and an apprentice willing to watch, fail, and try again. This was my first education in how wisdom actually moves between human beings — and why no manual or training program can replicate it.

"The knowledge that mattered most couldn't be found in a textbook. It had to be earned."
Pacific
Mariana Islands — Western Pacific

Where wisdom disappears quietly

Years living in the Mariana Islands showed me what happens when traditional knowledge transfer systems break down under political and economic pressure. Chamorro and Palauan communities preserving identity, language, and generational wisdom outside any formal institution — and losing it faster than anyone was documenting it.

"The most at-risk knowledge is the kind no one thinks to preserve until it's already gone."
Alaska
Birthplace of North American Heli-Ski · Coastal Communities

Where wisdom means survival

Four years based in the region recognized as the birthplace of the heli-ski industry — moving between extreme mountain environments and coastal fishing towns. In both worlds, expertise wasn't certified. It was inherited, passed person to person through seasons of proximity and hard-earned judgment. I watched what happened when the human chain of transmission broke. The lessons didn't transfer to manuals. They disappeared.

"In extreme environments, you stop pretending that formal knowledge is enough."
Nepal
Mountain Villages · Jungle Regions

Where wisdom is infrastructure

Time spent throughout Nepal's mountain villages and jungle regions deepened my understanding of human resilience and the ways communities preserve meaning through oral tradition and lived experience rather than institutional structure. Wisdom here wasn't a supplement to the system — it was the system.

"Some communities have always known what institutions are only now forgetting."
China
Rapidly Developing Cities · Traditional Communities

Where wisdom disappears at scale

Moving through rapidly developing cities while engaging with traditional food culture, education systems, and everyday social structures revealed the tension between modernization and cultural continuity at enormous scale. Generational knowledge, craftsmanship, and lived experience were disappearing not through neglect but through speed — societies evolving faster than the systems designed to preserve them.

"When a society moves faster than its memory, the loss is invisible until it's permanent."
Global
Thailand · Qatar · International Academic Communities

Where the solution took shape

Engagement with researchers, universities, and international academic communities across Thailand, Qatar, and beyond brought the pattern into focus. The problem was universal. The methodology needed to address it — structured, scalable, culturally adaptable — didn't exist. EdTerm was built to create it.

"Every culture has a different answer to the same question: how do you pass on what matters most?"
